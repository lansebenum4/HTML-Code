Football (Soccer)

The beautiful game - played by 250 million people across more than 200 countries.

A packed stadium watches a football match - the sport draws the largest crowds of any game on earth. Image source: Wikipedia

What Is Football?

Football - known as soccer in North America and Australia - is a sport played between two teams of eleven players on a rectangular grass pitch. The objective is to score by getting the ball into the opposing team's goal. Only the goalkeeper is allowed to use their hands. Every other player must use their feet, head, chest, or any other part of the body except the arms and hands to control and pass the ball.

A standard match consists of two 45-minute halves with a 15-minute break at half time. If the score is level at the end of normal time in a knockout competition, the match may go to extra time and then a penalty shootout. The team with the most goals at the end of the match wins.

Football is governed internationally by FIFA (the Federation Internationale de Football Association), which was founded in 1904 and today has 211 member associations - more members than the United Nations.

The Pitch and Basic Rules

A standard football pitch is between 100 and 110 metres long and 64 to 75 metres wide at the professional level. The goals are 7.32 metres wide and 2.44 metres high. Key rules include:

  • Players cannot deliberately handle the ball (except the goalkeeper in their own area)
  • A foul results in a free kick or penalty kick for the opposing team
  • A yellow card is a caution; two yellow cards or one red card means the player is sent off
  • The offside rule prevents attackers from being behind the last defender when the ball is played to them
  • A match is controlled by one referee and two assistant referees (linesmen)

A player lines up a free kick - set pieces are a major tactical element of the modern game. Image source: Wikipedia

History of Football

The Football Association, founded in London in 1863, wrote the first official rules of the game and established football as a distinct sport separate from rugby. The early years of organized football were dominated by English clubs, with the Football League - the world's first professional league - founded in 1888.

FIFA was founded in 1904, initially with just seven European member nations. The first FIFA World Cup was held in Uruguay in 1930, with 13 nations participating. Uruguay won the tournament on home soil. The World Cup has been held every four years since, with a pause only for World War II (1942 and 1946).

The post-war era saw football become a truly global sport. Brazil won five World Cups between 1958 and 2002, producing legendary players like Pele, Ronaldo, and Ronaldinho. European nations like Germany, Italy, France, and Spain each built dominant national teams. By the late 20th century, the sport had become the defining cultural institution of dozens of countries.

The Premier League Revolution

The English Premier League was founded in 1992, breaking away from the old Football League to negotiate a massive TV deal with BSkyB. The influx of television money transformed English football, attracting top players from around the world and turning clubs like Manchester United, Arsenal, Chelsea, and Liverpool into global brands worth billions of pounds.

Positions and Tactics

Modern football uses a variety of tactical formations, described by the number of players in each line from defence to attack. The most common is the 4-3-3, which uses four defenders, three midfielders, and three forwards. The 4-4-2 was dominant through the 1980s and 90s. More recent tactical trends favour a 4-2-3-1 or a 3-5-2.

Outfield Positions

  • Goalkeeper - Guards the goal, the only player allowed to use their hands
  • Centre-back - Defends the central areas in front of goal
  • Full-back - Defends the wide areas and supports attacks down the flanks
  • Defensive midfielder - Sits in front of the defence, breaking up opposition attacks
  • Central midfielder - Links defence and attack, covering large areas of the pitch
  • Winger - Operates on the flanks, providing pace and creativity
  • Striker - The primary goal scorer, leads the attack

Tactical preparation is now a central part of football at every level from grassroots to elite. Image source: Wikipedia

Major Competitions

Football's calendar is structured around a combination of domestic leagues and international tournaments. At club level, each country runs its own league competition. At international level, the FIFA World Cup and continental championships like UEFA Euro define the sport's biggest moments.

Football Around the World

In most of Europe, South America, Africa, and Asia, football is not just a sport - it is a cultural institution. In countries like Brazil, Argentina, Germany, and Italy, major matches bring everyday life to a standstill. Club rivalries like Real Madrid vs Barcelona (El Clasico) or Celtic vs Rangers (the Old Firm) carry deep social and sometimes political significance beyond sport.

In North America, football has historically competed with American football, basketball, and baseball for attention. However, the sport's profile has grown significantly in recent decades. The 1994 World Cup, hosted by the United States, drew record attendances. Major League Soccer (MLS) now operates 29 clubs and continues to expand. The arrival of global stars like David Beckham and, more recently, Lionel Messi to MLS has dramatically raised the league's profile.

Women's football has also grown enormously. The FIFA Women's World Cup, first held in 1991, drew a global television audience of over 1 billion for the 2019 edition. The United States has dominated the women's game, winning four World Cups. England's Lionesses won the UEFA Women's Euro in 2022, generating a wave of enthusiasm for the women's game in the UK.
